# Selection Policy

## Objective

Select the recent legal scholarship with the highest expected intellectual value, not the papers with the highest existing popularity. Prioritize AI-law, computational law, legal technology, algorithmic governance, data and platform regulation, and genuinely interdisciplinary legal work. Use excellent work from other legal fields to complete the digest only when it clears the same bar.

## Eligibility Gates

A candidate is eligible only when all of the following are true:

1. Its first public version, Online First publication, or issue publication is no more than 180 days before the report date.
2. It makes a substantive legal, institutional, regulatory, jurisprudential, or law-and-society contribution.
3. At least a meaningful abstract or executive summary is available.
4. Its venue or provenance satisfies `source-policy.md`, or there is a documented exceptional reason to admit it.
5. Its identity and date can be verified from a publisher, DOI record, repository, author institution, or issuing organization.
6. It has not appeared in `data/seen.json`.

Exclude news, blog posts, speeches, calls for papers, book reviews without an original thesis, purely descriptive client alerts, unreviewed student notes, and papers whose legal relevance is merely incidental.

## Internal Rubric

Score only to discipline judgment; never print the scores in the digest.

| Dimension | Range | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Frontier relevance | 0-5 | Does it address an emerging problem, new capability, new institution, or live doctrinal disruption? |
| Importance of the real problem | 0-5 | Would resolving the problem materially change rights, governance, institutions, or practice? |
| Rigor | 0-5 | Are the legal reasoning, evidence, methods, and counterarguments fit for the claim? |
| Novelty | 0-5 | Is the contribution more than repackaging a familiar position? |
| Legal significance | 0-5 | Does it change how law should be understood, made, applied, or studied? |
| Source/provenance | 0-4 | Does the venue or institution provide a credible quality signal? |
| Interdisciplinary leverage | 0-3 | Does another discipline genuinely improve the legal analysis? |
| Transparency | 0-2 | Are the text, data, assumptions, or methods inspectable? |

Do not include citation counts, downloads, Altmetric scores, author fame, or social-media attention. These measures are lagged or popularity-biased and are particularly unfair to new work.

## Ranking Rules

1. Rank by overall intellectual value, independent of language and country.
2. Break close ties in favor of a clearer real-world problem, stronger evidence, more consequential novelty, and better inspectability—in that order.
3. Do not impose Chinese/English quotas. Search both ecosystems, then let quality decide.
4. Select 2-5 when the evidence supports it. Selecting one or zero is valid and preferable to padding.
5. Keep the full digest readable in about 10 minutes.

## Selecting the Deep Read

Choose exactly one deep read when the report is nonempty. Normally select the highest-ranked work whose full text was inspected. If the apparent best work is abstract-only, either choose the next comparably valuable full-text work or keep the abstract-only work and explicitly constrain the analysis.

Apply bilateral steelmanning:

1. **Strongest author case:** reconstruct the most persuasive version of the paper's argument, including unstated premises only when clearly identified as reconstruction.
2. **Strongest objection:** formulate the most consequential, fair-minded challenge—not a weak objection the author can easily dismiss.
3. **Decisive crux:** identify the factual assumption, normative commitment, causal mechanism, institutional constraint, or legal interpretation on which the dispute turns.
4. **Verdict:** state what survives, what remains unproven, and why the paper is or is not worth close reading.

Criticism must be specific and calibrated to access. Never criticize absent methods or omitted arguments when only the abstract was available.
